# Bounded producer cohorts and output workers

WP05-S02-T04 completes the S02 qualification at the actual internal Core/Channel/memory-writer seam. Shipping sources, public APIs and dependencies are unchanged. The [fifteen shared cases](../../../conformance/logging/queue-burst-cases.json) seed an eligible record, wait for each interested writer's readiness, then release producer cohorts together. Each cohort joins before the next starts; writers stay held until admission observations are captured. Cleanup releases and joins outstanding producers and workers. No sleeps or scheduler-dependent caller order are used.

Capacities one, two and eight each cover console-only, file-only and both outputs with 64 producers. Two staged cases first admit Debug to one destination, then offer Warning to both: the first destination is full while the other's slots remain available. Both orientations execute. Disabled outputs, None and each enabled-but-uninterested threshold direction are included. An eligible lazy factory executes once even when admission rejects. When no output is interested, the factory and capture are skipped.

Literal expectations specify cohort outcome counts, per-output Accepted/Filtered/QueueFull counts, successful ticket totals, capture/factory calls and queue/worker observations. The identities of concurrent winners are intentionally not fixed. Both comparers independently classify each caller's output interest and aggregate outcome, require a unique contiguous successful-ticket prefix per output and bind every original wire record to its accepted caller. Every healthy accepted record must be attempted and written once in ticket order. Rejected callers cannot replace accepted records. Integer tickets are separate from the v1 event JSON-number equivalence policy.

At the held-writer observation, each output has at most capacity buffered slots and one in-flight callback. Pending maxima are sampled after producer calls; with seeded writers held, buffered counts cannot fall during the cohorts. Callback active maxima use atomic compare/exchange and include every callback entry, including the final drain. Source inspection separately confirms one cached sequential worker per enabled queue, no per-admission task and an awaited writer outside the admission gate. The combined accepted worker state is at most capacity plus one per output; disabled outputs have no queue/worker, and enabled-but-uninterested workers stay idle. This is not a global bound on caller-created producer tasks, pre-admission capture memory, allocations or the thread pool. Later flush coordination bookkeeping remains its own lifecycle task.

The shared transport checker now validates positive unique accepted tickets and null rejection tickets before caller-byte lookup. This makes malformed observations fail explicitly. C# and Python corruption controls reject excessive pending/active/worker observations, recapture, false outcomes, missing/gapped tickets, reordered delivery and internally consistent eviction bytes. The standalone Python matcher additionally checks exact timestamp and raw CR/framing. Both C# and Python process runners check managed/input identity and empty child directories; both burst layers check successful-ticket contiguity. There is no claim that every recorded corruption rejection names its branch or that the two matchers have identical standalone scope.

Physical console/file destination ownership, actual output/lifecycle composition, S03 output counters/diagnostics and complete flush/stop coordination are still pending and must preserve these invariants when integrated. No total delivery order across destinations or producer timestamps is promised. ConsoleHuman remains a scaffold.
