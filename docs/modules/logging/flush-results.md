# Public flush results

WP05-S08-T03 implements the actual CIS.Logging.CoreLogRuntime.FlushAsync(TimeSpan timeout, CancellationToken cancellationToken) method and its sealed getter-only result types. Public runtime creation and shutdown remain later tasks; development fixtures compose actual Core queues internally.

The timeout must be positive and finite. Invalid budgets throw ArgumentOutOfRangeException before registration or cancellation. Every positive TimeSpan is supported, including sub-millisecond and very long budgets. Registration captures each enabled output's accepted sequence and rejection counters plus global invalid/truncated counts. Already-cancelled callers still register the boundary, then receive OperationCanceledException carrying their own token. Cancellation and timeout detach the caller without erasing worker controls or interrupting output I/O.

One TimeProvider timer belongs to each active caller wait. Its remaining budget uses monotonic timestamp differences, with at most one day per timer slice for long values. Early timer callbacks recheck elapsed time. The same overall budget covers console and file; changing event UTC does not restart it. Timer scheduling has managed-runtime resolution and may overshoot a small budget. System UTC labels are captured with initial/final accounting under the admission gate, as selected in T01. The deadline callback freezes a consistent immutable observation before detaching its wait; later I/O and diagnostics cannot change it. Owned timer disposal joins active callbacks before disposing their cancellation source.

Completed means every covered record has a terminal disposition and every covered writer flush has resolved. It may report failure. TimedOut means covered work or flush confirmation was unresolved at its deadline observation. Succeeded requires all covered records written, no covered failure/abandonment/pending and all writer flushes confirmed. Empty outputs succeed. LossObserved includes initial invalid/truncated captures, initial queue-full/unavailable rejections, covered failure/abandonment and failed/unavailable writer flush confirmation. Filtering and unresolved pending alone do not confirm loss; Succeeded and LossObserved can both be true.

Each output preserves AcceptedSequenceBoundary = Written + Failed + Abandoned + Pending. Successful writes form a prefix; terminal failure records its one actual in-flight sequence, and abandonment records the exact queued tail. These bounded scalar facts exclude later admissions/failures and preserve a pending in-flight hole after admission exhaustion. Unavailable flush confirmation alone cannot declare that write completed. A lazy per-output progress signal wakes any such remaining boundary observers without keeping caller history.

Actual development qualification covers owned results, rejection/global snapshots, write/flush failures, caller cancellation, positive tiny/long budgets, UTC corrections, one combined destination budget, later failure exclusion, near-Int64 exhaustion and physical buffered JSONL visibility before shutdown. Near-exhaustion fixtures seed consistent prior counters; the single physical record is not misrepresented as historical writes. The system-timer scenario also exercises an actual .NET timer. A focused unit case holds an actually active .NET timer callback, cancels the caller, and verifies that asynchronous disposal remains pending until that callback is released. Long-budget fixtures assert the actual first callback and next one-day due time before cancellation. Controlled-clock callbacks test elapsed semantics without changing the operating-system clock. T04 retains broader concurrency, saturation, storage-bound stress and full S08 acceptance; file idle interval scheduling and public lifecycle remain later work.
